# Sales Deck Design Prompt — For SME Customers

**Audience:** a Nigerian SME owner deciding whether to sign up — think the "Chioma" persona from `PRD.md` §4: runs their business largely through WhatsApp/Instagram DMs, no developer, no patience for jargon, decides on trust, price, and "will this actually save me time" more than on technology. This deck is shown by a sales rep, sent as a WhatsApp attachment, or used on a tablet at a market/trade event — **not** read cold on a projector in a boardroom. Keep it short, visual, and in plain language throughout — no "Errand," no "architecture," no "TAM." If a slide needs a technical term to make sense, cut the term, not the slide.

**Brand & visual direction:** same brand as `DESIGN_PROMPT.md`/`DECK_DESIGN_PROMPT.md` — Kola (placeholder name), dark near-black base with one warm accent, editorial serif for big statements, clean sans for everything else — but turn the warmth up and the "pitch deck" formality down. More phone-mockup screenshots, fewer charts. Where the investor deck uses restraint, this deck can use one bold, friendly illustration per slide (a shop owner on their phone, a customer typing "how much?" — real-feeling, not corporate stock photography).

## Slide-by-Slide Structure (8–10 slides, short by design)

### Slide 1 — Cover
Just the name, a one-line promise in plain Nigerian-English register (e.g. *"Never miss a customer message again."*), and a phone-mockup hero image showing a WhatsApp conversation being answered instantly.

### Slide 2 — The Pain (said the way they'd say it, not the way a PRD says it)
Two or three short, first-person-feeling statements: *"Customers dey message you 24/7 but you fit only reply small small."* / *"You don sabi lose sales because nobody reply on time."* Use restrained Pidgin inflection if it fits your actual customer base's voice — test this with real target customers before locking the copy, tone can misfire if overdone. Visual: a simple phone showing a pile of unanswered messages.

### Slide 3 — The Fix, One Line
*"Kola answers your customers on WhatsApp — 24/7, in your own words, without hiring anyone."* No architecture talk. One supporting visual: before (owner overwhelmed) / after (owner relaxed, bot handling replies).

### Slide 4 — How It Works (3 steps, no more)
1. Tell us about your business (talk or upload a price list/FAQ)
2. We set up your bot in minutes
3. Connect your WhatsApp — you're live
Each step gets a single icon and a five-word label, not a paragraph.

### Slide 5 — What Your Bot Can Actually Do
Concrete, not abstract — pick 3 real scenarios matching the two MVP archetypes from `PRD.md` §6: answering FAQs/prices from an uploaded list, negotiating and confirming an order for a catalog business, escalating to the owner when it doesn't know the answer. Show each as a small chat-bubble mockup, not a bullet list.

### Slide 6 — See It In Action
One full-screen, realistic WhatsApp conversation mockup — customer asks a real question, bot answers naturally, ends in a sale or a resolved question. This slide should look almost exactly like a real WhatsApp screenshot; if you have one real conversation from testing, use it here instead of a mockup.

### Slide 7 — Pricing, In Naira, No Surprises
The plan cards from `DESIGN_PROMPT.md`'s pricing section, simplified: price, what's included, and the trial framing from `PRD.md` §10 in one plain sentence — *"Try everything free for 48 hours, then a lighter free version until day 14 — no card needed to start."* One line on WhatsApp's own message fees being passed through at cost, since a sales rep should be able to answer "will WhatsApp charge me too?" without fumbling.

### Slide 8 — Why Kola (light touch, not aggressive)
One calm comparison, not a competitor takedown: *"Other tools cost in dollars and expect you to build flowcharts. Kola just needs you to describe your business — in Naira, on WhatsApp, and Telegram too."* No competitor named by name in a customer-facing deck — that's for the investor/partner decks, not this one.

### Slide 9 — Getting Started
*"Takes about 5 minutes."* Show the actual first onboarding screen (per `DESIGN_PROMPT.md`'s onboarding wizard) so there's no mystery about what happens after they say yes.

### Slide 10 — Contact / CTA
A WhatsApp number or QR code to start a conversation right now (fits the channel — meet them where they already are), plus whatever contact the sales rep uses in person.
